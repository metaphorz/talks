AMELIA ISLAND · N ↑

Nine apps, each a model of one part of the island. Click a tile, or let it cycle; the pin shows where the model lives on the island.

All nine run in a browser. Most are plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step; Egans Creek uses Vite; the 3D ones use Three.js; the Live Oak can also drive Blender and Unreal on this computer.

When the Sea Slowed Down

1 · Geomorphology · the weld and “two piles of sand on a bed of mud”
Rate of sea-level rise · from the model’s curve
Plantation cross-section · west → east · schematic

Below about 2 mm a year, a barrier stops retreating and starts to build. That happened here about 5,000 years ago.

Top: the slope of the sea-level curve on the previous slide, in millimetres per year. Meltwater Pulse 1A runs at tens of mm/yr. Mariotti (2021) puts the switch from a retreating to a building barrier near 2 mm/yr; that falls near 5 ka, which matches Hopf’s date for the weld.

Growing a Coast from Waves

2 · Drumstick · a one-line Ashton–Murray shoreline, running live
Plan view · mainland left · Atlantic right0 yr
Wave rose · from the east
Climate diffusivity μ₂
+0.00

Positive: bumps smooth out.

Q = K·H12/5·T1/5·cos6/5(φ−θ)·sin(φ−θ)   ·   ∂y/∂t = −(1/D)·∂Q/∂x
CERC sand flux along the shore · the shoreline moves wherever that flux piles up or runs out

Drumstick is a physics companion to the geomorphology app: start with water and a nearly straight coast and ask what the waves build.

This is a small live version of its core, the Ashton–Murray one-line model. Each wave carries sand along the shore by the CERC formula; where flux converges, the shoreline advances.

The key idea: when most waves arrive at a high angle (more than 45° from shore-normal), the climate diffusivity goes negative and small bumps grow into capes. Hatteras does this; the Sea Islands, Amelia’s coast, stay smooth. Same four presets as the app.

The Egans Creek atlas has six chapters; the tide explorer is the most developed. It reads the latest NOAA observation at Fernandina Beach, every six minutes, and plots NOAA predictions for any date.

What you see here is its third mode, the labelled “illustrative cycle”: a 12.42-hour semidiurnal tide with the station’s 1.83 m mean range. Drag the chart or run the cycle. Mud banks are exposed and covered, the cordgrass floods, and the heron moves with the water’s edge.

Rules from the app: a short trunk that divides low into heavy limbs · near-horizontal limbs that sag, rest an elbow, and rise again · the trunk leans leeward with age · moss hangs on sheltered limbs, away from the salt face.

The Live Oak Explorer grows one coastal live oak from sliders. Its central idea is the skeleton: a graph of joints and links that every renderer then gives flesh.

This is a 2-D drawing of the same rules. Drag the compass or the sliders: the shear surface rises leeward at the crown shear angle; thin shoots that cross it die; thick limbs flatten under it. Age leans the trunk downwind. Moss avoids the windward face.

Light Through Moving Water

8 · WebGL Water, nozzle edition · caustics and black vortex discs
Pool floor · photons, live · click to add a wave
Nozzle
One mechanism, two effects

A crest is a converging lens: light piles up into a bright web. A vortex dimple is a diverging lens: light is thrown out of it, leaving a black disc.

The real thing

About a million photons a frame, refracted by Snell’s law and splatted onto the floor in WebGL2.

A reimplementation of Evan Wallace’s WebGL Water, with the sphere replaced by nozzles on the pool walls. A shallow nozzle makes waves, and waves make caustics. A deep nozzle spins a vortex, which shows on the floor as a black disc.

This canvas is a small CPU version of the photon idea: refract a grid of rays through the surface slope and add up where they land. Click to drop a wave. Switch to Deep to add a vortex dimple.

Shake the Sliders, Find the Bug

8 · Water · Gaussian variation and a boundary instability
Every slider is a mean

Roll a scene, keep your choice

Each control stores a z-score, not a value. The thumb never moves; repeated rolls can’t drift away from what you set.

v = clamp(μ + z·σ, vmin, vmax) · σ = 10% of range
Mean μ0.18
A picket fence of spikes

Measured, not tuned away

Spikes stood along the walls after tens of seconds. Read back from the GPU, wall vorticity was 17× the interior. A wall rule stamped after the pressure solve, and half-width stencils, fed a slow sink: every mode decays at √d.

Before · Corner Whirl, 70 s
After · ghost-cell walls

Left: the design-gallery idea. Each slider is the mean of a Gaussian; Randomize draws a z-score for it. Move the mean: the cloud of draws moves with it. Near an end stop the clipped draws pile up at the bound, and the app shows that honestly.

Right: a bug found by measuring. |λ| = √0.99925 for every mode means a 22-second e-folding time, far too slow to absorb a source acting every step. Fix: free-slip walls as mirrored ghost cells.

Breeding Victorian Houses

9 · Victorian House Simulator · a genetic algorithm over house DNA
Selected genome
Statistical map · 53 houses

Gene odds come from photos read by a vision model.

Click a house, then

The simulator shows a 3×3 grid of procedurally built 3D houses. Each house is a genome: style, stories, roof, turret, porch, cladding, palette. Breed keeps the selected parent and fills the other eight with mutants; Crossover mixes two parents; locks hold genes fixed.

The odds aren’t made up: about 100 Wikimedia photos were analysed by a vision model through OpenRouter; 53 confirmed Victorians gave the rates, such as one-over-one windows 78%, turrets 28%, mansard roofs 31%. These elevations are a 2-D sketch of each genome.

What Each Model Leaves Out

Every model is a choice · in their own words
Geomorphology

Paleo-shores are offsets

The 12, 8 and 5 ka shorelines are interpretive sketches from migration distances, labelled as such.

Drumstick

Not Amelia’s outline

No antecedent topography, no storm-by-storm detail; inlets are parameterized, not resolved.

Dunes

Slabs, not grains

Rules on a grid stand in for wind over sand; the continuum version is still adding moisture and plants.

Egans Creek

No creek gauge

Fernandina Beach is a nearby reference. No invented lag, no bathtub flooding forecast.

Tree Atlas

Records, not trees

Not a census of the island. Nearby points are candidates for review, not confirmed duplicates.

Live Oak

One tree, typical traits

Salt aerosol is invisible in life, so its overlay is off by default. The Meshy model is reference only.

Wormstone

No unseen interior

A geometric approximation: no organism, mineral or erosion is inferred from the photograph.

Water

A pool, not the sea

A shallow-water heightfield plus 2-D flow; the vortex slider’s default stays at the physical value.

Victorian

53 houses, not a town

Gene odds from a Commons sample; procedural textures stand in where no asset fits.

A model is useful because of what it leaves out, as long as it says so.
