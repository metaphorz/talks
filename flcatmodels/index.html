A Hurricane Model Is a Chain

Each link is one of the disciplines on the Professional Team

Every accepted model in Florida has all four links. Each of our apps opens one or more of them up.

Tracks (meteorology and statistics): where storms go and how often. Wind (meteorology): how strong the wind is at each place. Damage (vulnerability): what that wind does to a structure. Loss (actuarial): what that damage costs across many policies.

[builds 1–4] The four apps, each under the links it covers: meteoearth shows the atmosphere that steers storms; the Multimodel spans the whole chain.

1 · meteoearth

The atmosphere a hurricane lives in, today
What it is

Today’s weather on a globe

An interactive Earth: drag to turn it, scroll to zoom, and watch the wind move as animated particles. Switch the layer underneath to temperature, humidity, pressure, cloud, gusts, rain or storm energy.

Four global forecast models
  • GFS (NOAA, USA) · IFS (ECMWF, Europe) · GEM (Environment Canada) · ICON (DWD, Germany)
  • Each one’s latest run, from now to two days ahead, every 3 hours.
  • Rebuilt automatically every 12 hours on GitHub, then published as a static site.

For hurricanes: the large-scale winds steer a storm, and warm, moist air feeds it.

4global forecast models
10layers, from 10 m wind to the jet stream
12 hbetween automatic refreshes

The drawing is a cartoon of what the globe shows with real data: trade winds blowing west in the tropics, westerlies to the north, and a hurricane turning in between.

Before any catastrophe model: the weather itself. The same four agencies' models are what forecasters use every day.

2 · Synthetic Tracks

Made-up hurricanes that behave like real ones
The problem

History is too short

Florida has had only about 80 landfalls since 1960. That is too few to judge a 1-in-100-year storm, so models invent thousands more that behave like the real ones.

How it works · CLIPER-Analog
  • CLIPER (climatology and persistence): “keep going the way you are, bent by the winds typical for this place and season.”
  • Analogs: the eight past storms most like this one add their real wobbles, so the tracks fan out realistically.
  • Ocean warmth caps strength; land weakens it. A rebuilt Hall & Jewson (2007) model gives a second opinion.
25,000synthetic storms in about 4 seconds
0.88–1.09track spread vs. real errors (1 is ideal)
−11%Florida landfalls vs. the record

The drawing: a storm is followed up to a cutoff, then the model takes over and draws a spread of possible continuations. Some come ashore in Florida.

Every design choice was judged against pass/fail tests written down before the results were seen. It is honest about its misses: 11% too few Florida landfalls, 25% too few hurricane landfalls, and it is not a forecaster.

3 · Storm Animator

The wind around a moving storm, hour by hour
The question

How strong is the wind, here, now?

A track gives the centre. Damage depends on the wind at each house, which rises to a peak at the radius of maximum wind and falls off outside it.

Three windfield models
  • Holland: a pressure profile with a shape parameter B, turned into wind by gradient balance.
  • Powell: a boundary-layer (slab) PDE solved for the whole field, with surface drag. The physical reference.
  • Willoughby: a power law inside the eye wall, exponential decay outside.

The storm’s own motion adds to the wind on its right and subtracts on its left.

Holland B

19hourly frames of Charley, 2004

The drawing is live: two wind profiles from the centre outward, and a storm moving across the top showing the stronger right side. Change Holland's B: a larger B gives a sharper peak and faster fall-off.

4 · Pro Team Multimodel

The whole chain, on the Commission’s own test grid
The experiment · Form S-6

One grid, 840 points, a storm going west

The Commission’s Form S-6 sets a 21 × 40 grid over South Florida, about 3 miles apart, and a storm that starts 9 miles offshore and moves due west.

  • Wind: Powell, Holland or Willoughby, with land roughness and inland decay.
  • Damage and loss: a damage curve and an exposure book turn peak wind into dollars at every point.
  • Statistics: 200 storms, then sensitivity and uncertainty: which inputs drive the loss?

Finding: sampled realistically, central pressure is the dominant driver of loss.

840grid vertices, 682 on land
200storms, with realistic links between inputs
5disciplines, each swappable

The drawing: the actual Form S-6 grid shape (40 columns, 21 rows) with the track along the row through landfall. As the storm passes, each point keeps its peak wind; colours run from calm to severe.

The tool is built for the Professional Team: every discipline's choice can be swapped and its effect seen.
